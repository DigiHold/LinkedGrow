# LinkedIn DM Outreach Guide

> **MANDATORY: Follow this guide every time the user says "write dm" followed by a LinkedIn profile URL.**

---

## Step 1: Research the Person

You CANNOT scrape LinkedIn directly (returns 999). Instead, run ALL of these in parallel:

### Web Searches (run all 3)
```
"{Full Name}" LinkedIn creator
"{Full Name}" LinkedIn coach OR ghostwriter OR consultant
"{Full Name}" interview OR podcast OR newsletter
```

### Extract their name from the URL
LinkedIn URLs follow `/in/username/` format. Use the username to find their real name from search results.

### Fetch their website (if found)
If search results reveal a personal site, fetch it with WebFetch to get:
- Their story and background
- Services they offer
- Their tone of voice and personality
- Client types and audience
- Any personal details (location, hobbies, values)

### Fetch any interviews/articles about them
Interviews reveal personality, opinions, and specific quotes you can reference. Prioritize these over generic bio pages.

---

## Step 2: Build the Person Profile

Before writing anything, compile this info:

| Field | What to find |
|-------|-------------|
| **Name** | First name only for the DM |
| **Niche** | What they do (coaching, ghostwriting, SaaS, freelancing, etc.) |
| **Audience** | Who they serve (founders, creators, executives, etc.) |
| **Content style** | How they write (direct, storytelling, data-driven, casual, etc.) |
| **Something specific** | A recent post topic, podcast appearance, article, unique take, or achievement |
| **Why LinkedGrow fits** | How their audience would benefit from LinkedGrow specifically |
| **Partnership angle** | What type of partnership makes sense (affiliate, sponsorship, collaboration) |

---

## Step 3: Ask the User for Context

Before writing the DM, ask:

1. **What's the goal?** (affiliate partnership, sponsorship, collaboration, feedback, something else)
2. **Any specific angle?** (or should I propose one based on research)

If the user already specified the goal in their message (e.g., "write dm: URL - affiliate partnership"), skip asking and proceed.

---

## Step 4: Write the DM

### Golden Rules

These are non-negotiable. Every DM MUST follow ALL of these:

**Length:**
- 50-100 words. Short enough to read in 10 seconds, long enough to show you're a real person who did homework.
- 4-6 sentences max.

**Structure (4 parts):**
1. **Title/Subject line** - LinkedIn DMs can have a title. Use it. Something direct like "Would you be open to a partnership?" or "Quick question about [their niche]". Not clickbait, just honest.
2. **Disarming opener** (1 sentence) - Acknowledge reality. These people get flooded with DMs. Saying something like "you probably get 100 DMs daily for partnerships so I'll be brief" is honest, human, and immediately makes you stand out from the people pretending they're the only one reaching out.
3. **Context + curiosity gap** (2-3 sentences) - Show you actually know their work ("been following your stuff for a while, love what you propose with [specific thing]"), then hint at what you're building without over-explaining. Mention their clients/audience as the connection point. Keep it vague enough that they need to reply to learn more.
4. **Soft CTA** (1 sentence) - "Happy to share more if you're curious!" or "Would you be open to exploring it?". Low commitment, easy to reply to.

**Tone:**
- Write like a real person texting someone they respect but don't know yet
- Casual but not sloppy
- Confident but not pushy
- NO em dashes (use commas or dashes with spaces instead)
- NO words: "resonate", "leverage", "excited", "thrilled", "amazing", "incredible", "game-changer", "unique opportunity", "I'd love to pick your brain"
- NO exclamation marks overload (1 max in the entire DM)
- Vary sentence length - mix short and longer ones

**What NEVER to include in the first DM:**
- Product name (unless they'd recognize it)
- What the product does
- Features or pricing
- The word "affiliate" or "commission" (too transactional for a first message)
- Links of any kind
- Your full title or company pitch
- Paragraphs of text

**What to DO:**
- Make them curious enough to ask "what is it?" or "tell me more"
- Show you actually looked at their profile (specific reference, not "love your content")
- Frame it around THEIR audience or clients benefiting
- Keep it so short they can't NOT read it
- ALWAYS end the DM with a sign-off: "Best,\nNicolas Lecocq"

---

## Step 5: Prepare a Follow-Up (If They Don't Reply)

Write a follow-up DM to send 5-7 days later if no reply. Rules:

- Even shorter than the first DM (2-3 sentences max)
- Don't repeat the first message
- Add a small new piece of value or context
- Keep it light and zero-pressure
- Example angle: reference something they posted recently as a natural reason to bump

---

## Step 6: Prepare a Response If They Say No

If they decline, respond with:

1. **Be gracious** - Thank them, keep it short, no guilt-tripping
2. **Do NOT use the "do you know someone who..." technique** - Research shows this backfires. It signals you expected the no and were using them as a stepping stone. It feels manipulative and damages any chance of a future relationship.
3. **Leave the door open naturally** - A simple "no worries, wishing you the best" is 10x better than trying to squeeze a referral out of a rejection. If there's ever a fit in the future, they'll remember you were cool about it.

---

## Step 7: Present to the User

Show the user:

1. **Person summary** (2-3 lines - who they are, why they're a good fit)
2. **The DM** (final version, ready to copy-paste)
3. **Follow-up DM** (in case no reply after a week)

Do NOT show the research process, profile table, or internal reasoning. Just the clean output.

## Anti-Patterns (What Bad DMs Look Like)

These are real examples of what NOT to do. If your DM reads like any of these, rewrite it:

**Too long / too much info:**
> Hey! I'm Nicolas, founder of LinkedGrow. We're an AI-powered LinkedIn content tool that uses a BYOK model where users bring their own API key. We support 26 models including GPT-5, Claude, Gemini. Your clients would save 90% compared to Taplio. I'd love to set up an affiliate partnership with 30% recurring commission...

**Generic flattery:**
> Hey, I love your content! You're doing amazing work on LinkedIn. I have something that could really help your audience...

**Too salesy:**
> I have an exclusive opportunity for top LinkedIn creators like you. Our affiliate program offers industry-leading commissions and your audience is the perfect fit...

**The fake referral ask:**
> If this isn't for you, do you know anyone who might be interested?

**What a GOOD DM looks like:**

> **Title: Would you be open to a partnership?**
>
> Hey Shoaib, you probably get 100 daily DMs for partnerships so I'll be brief.
>
> Been following your stuff for a while, and I love what you propose, I'm working on a LinkedIn content tool that takes a completely different approach to pricing than competitors, and I keep thinking your coaching and ghostwriting clients are the exact people who'd benefit most from it. I'd love to explore some kind of partnership if you're open to it.
>
> Happy to share more if you're curious!

Why this works:
- Acknowledges they're busy and get spammed (honest, disarming)
- Shows familiarity with their work without fake flattery
- Mentions their clients as the fit (it's about them, not you)
- "Completely different approach" creates curiosity without explaining
- Ends easy to reply to

Only use these AFTER they reply and ask what it is. Never in the first DM.
