## What is Web Page to LinkedIn?

Web Page to LinkedIn lets you turn any web page into a LinkedIn post. This covers everything that is not a blog, Reddit thread, or YouTube video - news articles, product pages, press releases, research reports, company announcements, landing pages, documentation, and anything else with readable text content.

## How to use it

### Step 1 - Paste a URL

Go to **Repurpose** in the sidebar and paste any web page URL. If it does not match Reddit, YouTube, or a known blog platform, LinkedGrow treats it as a web page and extracts the text content.

### Step 2 - Choose a hook

The AI reads the page content and generates multiple hooks - different angles for your LinkedIn post. Each hook highlights a different takeaway from the page. Pick the one that fits your message.

### Step 3 - Pick a post variation

Based on your hook, the AI generates 3 post variations. Select the one you want to work with.

### Step 4 - Edit and publish

The post opens in the editor where you can refine, add images, save as draft, schedule, or publish immediately.

## What types of web pages work well

- **News articles** - Industry news from TechCrunch, Forbes, The Verge, etc. Share your take on what the news means for your audience.
- **Product pages** - A new tool or feature launch that is relevant to your industry. Break down what it does and why it matters.
- **Research reports** - Pull a key finding from a report and explain what it means in practice.
- **Press releases** - Company announcements that affect your industry. Add your perspective on what it signals.
- **Documentation** - Technical docs that explain a concept your audience should know about.

## Tips for web page repurposing

- **Add your perspective.** A LinkedIn post that just summarizes a web page is not valuable. Tell your audience what the page means, why it matters, or what they should do about it.
- **Focus on one insight.** Web pages often contain many points. Pick the single most interesting one and build your post around it.
- **Keep the link out of the body.** LinkedIn's algorithm deprioritizes posts with external links. Share the URL in the first comment instead.
