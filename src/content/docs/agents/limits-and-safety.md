Speed is what gets LinkedIn accounts restricted, and everything below exists to keep your agent boring to look at.

## Your own address

Each LinkedIn account gets one residential address in its own country, reserved for that account for as long as you have it. It is never shared with another customer and never rotated, so to LinkedIn your account looks like a person at home with one steady connection.

## A pace a person would keep

The agent works inside your business hours, in your time zone, and leaves real gaps between actions. No weekends, nothing at three in the morning, and no bursts. A new agent starts with a handful of actions a day and earns its way up over its first weeks.

You cannot turn the warm-up off, and that is deliberate. An account that goes from zero to full pace in a day is the single easiest thing for LinkedIn to spot.

## It stops rather than pushes

A security check, an unusual screen, a password that no longer works, a limit getting close: the agent stops, records exactly what it saw, and waits for you. You get it on the dashboard and by email.

It never retries into a checkpoint, because retrying is what turns a short interruption into a restricted account.

## Nobody is contacted twice

Every lead your agents have touched is remembered across all of them. Two of your agents never write to the same person, and nobody who already replied is approached again by a different agent.

## What you can stop at any time

- Pause an agent from its page. It finishes nothing in flight and starts nothing new.
- Take over any conversation and the agent stops writing to that person.
- Disconnect the LinkedIn account and everything stops immediately. Your leads and conversation history stay where they are.

## If the payment fails

Agents are paused rather than deleted. The LinkedIn activity stops, and your leads, sequences and history are all still there when the payment goes through.
